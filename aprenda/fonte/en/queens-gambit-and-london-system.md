The Queen's Gambit (**1.d4 d5 2.c4**) and the London System (**1.d4** with an early bishop to f4) are the two most popular ways to play 1.d4. In the Queen's Gambit, White offers the c4 pawn to lure Black's d5 pawn away from the center. In the London, White builds nearly the same setup every game (d4, Bf4, e3, c3, Nf3, Nbd2 and Bd3) and depends very little on what Black does. The first is a fight for the center with plenty of theory; the second is a system that trades theory for plans.

## Queen's Gambit: 1.d4 d5 2.c4 {#gambito-da-dama "Queen's Gambit"}

```tabuleiro
fen: rnbqkbnr/ppp1pppp/8/3p4/2PP4/8/PP2PPPP/RNBQKBNR b KQkq c3 0 2
setas: c4d5
legenda: The Queen's Gambit after 2.c4: the c4 pawn attacks d5, Black's central pawn.
```

Despite the name, it isn't a true gambit. If Black takes on c4, White usually wins the pawn back with e3 and Bxc4 (or with Qa4+). The real point of 2.c4 is to **swap a wing pawn (c) for a central one (d)** and gain more control of the center.

Black has three main replies, each with its own established name.

## Queen's Gambit Accepted: 2...dxc4 {#aceito "Queen's Gambit Accepted"}

Black takes the pawn without trying to keep it, and gets free development in return. The main line goes **3.Nf3 Nf6 4.e3 e6 5.Bxc4 c5 6.O-O a6**.

- **White:** regain the pawn, develop quickly and play in the center (Qe2, Rd1, sometimes e4). White often ends up with an isolated pawn on d4, which comes with active piece play.
- **Black:** ...b5 and ...Bb7, pressure on d4 with ...cxd4, and a solid endgame if pieces come off.

Another way to meet it is **3.e4**, grabbing the center right away.

What Black should **not** do is try to hang on to the c4 pawn with ...b5. The opening's best-known trap shows why (see the exercise below).

## Queen's Gambit Declined: 2...e6 {#recusado "Queen's Gambit Declined"}

With 2...e6, Black supports d5 with a pawn and holds the center. The structure is very solid, and the price is the **c8 bishop**, locked behind e6, the same problem as in the [French Defense](artigo:francesa-e-caro-kann).

```tabuleiro
fen: rnbq1rk1/ppp1bppp/4pn2/3p2B1/2PP4/2N1PN2/PP3PPP/R2QKB1R b KQ - 2 6
setas: g5f6:vermelha
legenda: Queen's Gambit Declined after 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 O-O 6.Nf3: White puts pressure on the f6 knight, the defender of d5.
```

The classical line is **3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Be7 5.e3 O-O 6.Nf3**, the basis of the **Orthodox Defense** (with ...Nbd7). The plans:

- **White:** Rc1, Bd3 and pressure down the c-file; the g5 bishop keeps the f6 knight, the defender of d5, under tension.
- **Black:** free the game with ...dxc4 followed by ...c5 or ...e5, or trade pieces with ...h6 and ...Ne4. Everything aims at bringing the c8 bishop to life.

### Exchange Variation: 4.cxd5 exd5

White trades on d5 early: **4.cxd5 exd5 5.Bg5 c6 6.e3 Be7 7.Bd3**. The typical plan is the **[minority attack](artigo:estrutura-de-peoes)**: White's b-pawn (b4-b5) advances against Black's queenside majority to create a weak pawn on c6. In return, Black's c8 bishop is free.

## Slav Defense: 2...c6 {#eslava "Slav Defense"}

In the **Slav**, Black defends d5 with the c-pawn, keeping the c8 bishop's diagonal open.

```tabuleiro
fen: rn1qkb1r/pp2pppp/2p2n2/5b2/P1pP4/2N2N2/1P2PPPP/R1BQKB1R w KQkq - 1 6
setas: c8f5:azul
legenda: Slav after 3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 dxc4 5.a4 Bf5: Black has taken on c4 and developed the bishop before ...e6.
```

The main line is **3.Nf3 Nf6 4.Nc3 dxc4 5.a4 Bf5**. Black only takes the pawn once development is under way; a4 stops ...b5, and White wins c4 back later. When Black combines ...c6 with ...e6, the opening becomes the more complex **Semi-Slav**.

## London System {#london "London System"}

The **London System** is a system opening: White builds the same setup against almost anything and saves both clock time and study time.

```tabuleiro
fen: r1bq1rk1/pp3ppp/2nbpn2/2pp4/3P4/2PBPNB1/PP1N1PPP/R2QK2R b KQ - 6 8
setas: f3e5 f2f4:azul
legenda: London System after 1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 Nf6 3.e3 e6 4.Nf3 c5 5.c3 Nc6 6.Nbd2 Bd6 7.Bg3 O-O 8.Bd3: the setup is complete; typical plans involve Ne5 and f4.
```

The moves are almost always the same: **d4, Bf4, e3, Nf3, c3, Nbd2, Bd3 and castling**. The bishop goes to f4 **before** e3 so it doesn't get shut in behind the pawn chain. The plans:

- **White:** Ne5 with f4 to attack on the kingside; the e4 break when the center allows it. Against ...Bd6, the retreat Bg3 keeps the bishop, and if Black trades on g3, the h-file opens after hxg3.
- **Black:** ...c5 with ...Qb6, hitting d4 and b2 at the same time (the usual reply is Qb3); ...Bd6 to trade off the f4 bishop; ...Nh5 to hunt that same bishop.

The London's strength is economy: you learn one structure and its typical plans, not trees of variations. The downside is that against well-prepared opponents the position tends to end up equal without much of a fight.

## Typical traps {#armadilhas "Typical traps"}

### Queen's Gambit Accepted: holding the pawn with ...b5

```exercicio
fen: rnbqkbnr/p3pppp/8/1p6/2pP4/4P3/1P3PPP/RNBQKBNR w KQkq - 0 6
enunciado: After 2...dxc4 3.e3 b5? 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5, White wins material with a queen move. Which one?
solucao: d1f3
dica: Black's b- and c-pawns have left the long diagonal. What's sitting at the end of it, on a8?
explicacao: 6.Qf3! hits the a8 rook along the f3-a8 diagonal. If Black puts a piece in the way, it falls: 6...Nc6 7.Qxc6+ or 6...Bb7 7.Qxb7. Trying to keep the gambit pawn has cost a piece.
```

### Queen's Gambit Declined: the Elephant Trap

In the Declined, the f6 knight looks [pinned](artigo:cravada) by the g5 bishop, so White sometimes thinks the d5 pawn is free. It isn't.

```exercicio
fen: r1bqkb1r/pppn1ppp/5n2/3N2B1/3P4/8/PP2PPPP/R2QKBNR b KQkq - 0 6
enunciado: After 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.Bg5 Nbd7 5.cxd5 exd5 6.Nxd5??, Black wins a piece. Find the move that starts it all.
solucao: f6d5 g5d8 f8b4 d1d2 b4d2 e1d2 e8d8
dica: The pin is fake. If the bishop takes your queen, a check wins White's queen back.
explicacao: 6...Nxd5! 7.Bxd8 Bb4+ 8.Qd2 Bxd2+ 9.Kxd2 Kxd8 — Black gives the queen back but ends up a piece up for a pawn. This is the Elephant Trap.
```

:::aviso
**⚠️ Pinned doesn't mean frozen:** a piece pinned to the queen (not to the king) can still move, as long as the opponent pays a high price for taking the queen. Always check what happens after you "win" the queen.
:::

## Which to choose: Queen's Gambit or London? {#qual-escolher "Which to choose"}

- **Queen's Gambit:** for players who want to fight for an advantage with 1.d4 and are willing to [study Black's replies](artigo:preparacao-de-aberturas) (Accepted, Declined and Slav). It teaches classical strategy: structures, open files, the isolated pawn.
- **London System:** for players with little study time, who play a lot of blitz or want the same positions every game.

Plenty of players start with the London and switch to the Queen's Gambit when they want more ambitious positions. If you're building your first repertoire, also compare the options in the [openings guide for beginners](artigo:aberturas-para-iniciantes).

:::pratique Build your 1.d4 repertoire
Play White against the computer starting with 1.d4. Alternate between 2.c4 and 2.Bf4 for a few games and see which structure feels better.
:::

## Frequently asked questions {#faq "FAQ"}

### Does the Queen's Gambit lose a pawn?

Not really. If Black takes on c4, White usually wins it back. Trying to keep it with ...b5 tends to cost material.

### Is the London System good for beginners?

Yes. It needs little memorization, gives solid positions and teaches clear plans. The risk is playing on autopilot and ignoring what your opponent is doing.

### What's the difference between the Slav and the Semi-Slav?

In the Slav, Black plays ...c6 and keeps the c8 bishop free to come out. In the Semi-Slav, Black combines ...c6 with ...e6, a more closed structure with more theory.

### Why does the bishop go to f4 before e3 in the London?

Because after e3 the c1 bishop would be locked behind the pawn. It gets out first, then the pawn chain closes.
