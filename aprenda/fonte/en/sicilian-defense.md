The Sicilian Defense is the reply **1...c5** to 1.e4: instead of occupying the center with a pawn, Black attacks the d4 square from the side. The result is an unbalanced position where White usually attacks on the kingside and Black counterattacks along the c-file and on the queenside. It is one of the most played defenses against 1.e4 at every level, and the choice of players who want to win with Black, not just hold a draw.

## The idea: imbalance from move one {#ideia "The idea"}

Against 1.e4 e5, White knows where the pieces belong. Against 1...c5, the structure changes. The most typical game is the **Open Sicilian**, where White plays Nf3 and d4 and trades the d-pawn for Black's c-pawn.

:::caixa 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3
The starting point of the Open Sicilian. White has more space, quick development and the d-file. Black has the half-open c-file and **two central pawns (d and e) against one** (e4), which gives a sound structure for the endgame.
:::

That trade shapes the plans:

- **White:** develop quickly, often castle queenside and storm Black's king with the f-, g- and h-pawns.
- **Black:** press down the c-file, expand with ...a6 and ...b5, and cash in on the extra central pawn once the dust settles.

The price is theory. White has the initiative and the lines are concrete; in the Sicilian a lost tempo hurts more than in closed openings.

## Najdorf: 5...a6 {#najdorf "Najdorf"}

The **Najdorf** is one of the most famous Sicilian lines. 5...a6 looks slow but it's flexible: it controls b5 (keeping White's knights and bishop off it) and prepares ...e5 or ...e6, followed by ...b5.

```tabuleiro
fen: rnbqkb1r/1p2pppp/p2p1n2/8/3NP3/2N5/PPP2PPP/R1BQKB1R w KQkq - 0 6
setas: a6b5 b7b5:azul
legenda: The Najdorf after 5...a6: the pawn covers b5 and prepares to expand with ...b5.
```

White has several systems. The most common are:

- **6.Be3**, the **English Attack**: f3, Qd2, queenside castling and a g4-g5 pawn storm.
- **6.Bg5**: active development, often followed by f4.
- **6.Be2**: a more positional approach with kingside castling.

The Najdorf takes work. In return, it gives Black a rich position to play for a win.

## Dragon: 5...g6 {#dragao "Dragon"}

In the **Dragon**, Black fianchettoes the bishop on g7, where it sweeps the long a1-h8 diagonal and presses on d4 and White's queenside.

```tabuleiro
fen: rnbqkb1r/pp2pp1p/3p1np1/8/3NP3/2N5/PPP2PPP/R1BQKB1R w KQkq - 0 6
setas: f8g7:azul
legenda: The Dragon after 5...g6: the bishop is heading for g7, on the long diagonal.
```

White's most aggressive answer is the **Yugoslav Attack**: 6.Be3 Bg7 7.f3 O-O 8.Qd2 Nc6, then 9.Bc4 or 9.O-O-O. With opposite-side castling it becomes a race:

- **White:** h4-h5 to open the h-file, and Bh6 to trade off the g7 bishop, the king's main defender.
- **Black:** ...Rc8, a knight on c4 (via e5) and, often, the [exchange sacrifice](artigo:sacrificios) ...Rxc3 to wreck White's castled king.

The move 7.f3 has a clear job: stop ...Ng4, which would hit the e3 bishop. Move order matters here, as the exercise further down shows.

## Sveshnikov: 2...Nc6 and 5...e5 {#sveshnikov "Sveshnikov"}

In the **Sveshnikov**, Black accepts permanent weaknesses in exchange for activity.

```tabuleiro
fen: r1bqkb1r/pp1p1ppp/2n2n2/4p3/3NP3/2N5/PPP2PPP/R1BQKB1R w KQkq e6 0 6
setas: e5d4:vermelha
casas: d5
legenda: Sveshnikov after 2...Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 e5: the pawn kicks the knight, but d5 loses its pawn protection.
```

The main line runs **6.Ndb5 d6 7.Bg5 a6 8.Na3 b5**. Black ends up with a backward pawn on d6 and a hole on d5. In return Black gains time against White's knights and gets dynamic play. White's plan is easy to state and hard to carry out: control d5 with pieces.

## Anti-Sicilians: Alapin, Closed and Smith-Morra {#anti-sicilianas "Anti-Sicilians"}

Plenty of White players dodge Open Sicilian theory on move 2:

- **Alapin (2.c3):** prepares d4 so White can recapture with a pawn and keep an e4-d4 center. The main replies are 2...d5 and 2...Nf6.
- **Closed Sicilian (2.Nc3):** with g3 and Bg2, White keeps the center closed and plays on the kingside.
- **Smith-Morra Gambit (2.d4 cxd4 3.c3):** White gives a pawn for development and open lines.
- Against 2...Nc6 there's the **Rossolimo** (3.Bb5); against 2...d6, the **Moscow Variation** (3.Bb5+).

```tabuleiro
fen: rnbqkbnr/pp1ppppp/8/2p5/4P3/2P5/PP1P1PPP/RNBQKBNR b KQkq - 0 2
setas: d2d4
legenda: The Alapin after 2.c3: the pawn prepares d4, so White can recapture on d4 with a pawn.
```

If you play the Sicilian as Black, you need a ready answer to each of these. It doesn't have to be long; a clear plan is enough.

## Typical Sicilian traps {#armadilhas "Typical traps"}

### Dragon: the premature ...Ng4

If Black plays ...Ng4 before ...Bg7, the move seems to win the **bishop pair** (keeping both bishops against bishop and knight), since the knight hits e3. But the g4 knight is defended only by the c8 bishop, and one check changes everything.

```exercicio
fen: rnbqkb1r/pp2pp1p/3p2p1/8/3NP1n1/2N1B3/PPP2PPP/R2QKB1R w KQkq - 2 7
enunciado: After 5...g6 6.Be3 Ng4?, White wins material. What's the move?
solucao: f1b5
dica: The g4 knight is only defended by the c8 bishop. A check forces Black to block on the b5-e8 diagonal.
explicacao: 7.Bb5+! Bd7 8.Qxg4 — the d7 bishop is pinned by the b5 bishop and can't recapture on g4. If 7...Nd7, the knight closes the c8-g4 diagonal and 8.Qxg4 wins the piece anyway; if 7...Qd7, then 8.Bxd7+ wins the queen. The toughest defense is 7...Nc6, which blocks the check without taking the c8 bishop off g4: 8.Nxc6 bxc6 9.Bxc6+ Bd7 10.Bxa8 Qxa8, and White wins the exchange and a pawn.
```

### Smith-Morra: the Siberian Trap

In the Smith-Morra Gambit, the best-known trap works for Black. White plays natural moves and the queen on e2 becomes a target.

```exercicio
fen: r1b1kb1r/ppqp1ppp/2n1p3/8/2B1P1n1/2N2N1P/PP2QPP1/R1B2RK1 b kq - 0 9
enunciado: After 2.d4 cxd4 3.c3 dxc3 4.Nxc3 Nc6 5.Nf3 e6 6.Bc4 Qc7 7.O-O Nf6 8.Qe2 Ng4 9.h3?, Black wins. Find the move.
solucao: c6d4
dica: Only the f3 knight defends h2. Attack the queen in a way that makes taking your piece cost White the king.
explicacao: 9...Nd4! hits the queen on e2. If 10.Nxd4??, then 10...Qh2#, because h2 has lost its only defender. If the queen moves (10.Qd1, 10.Qd3, 10.Qe3), 10...Nxf3+ removes that defender and 11...Qh2# follows. The best defense is 10.hxg4 Nxe2+ 11.Nxe2, but White is left with two knights for the queen.
```

:::aviso
**⚠️ The pattern behind both traps:** a piece that looks defended but has only one defender. Before playing a natural move, count who guards each important square near the kings.
:::

## Who should play the Sicilian Defense {#para-quem "Who it's for"}

- **It's for you** if you enjoy unbalanced positions, don't mind studying theory and want to play for a win with Black.
- **Think twice** if you prefer calm positions or don't have time to study: the [French or the Caro-Kann](artigo:francesa-e-caro-kann) give you solid positions with less forcing theory.
- **Start simple:** pick just one line (the Dragon has plans that are easier to grasp; the Najdorf and the Sveshnikov need more theory) and prepare an answer to the Alapin, the Closed and the Smith-Morra. Don't try to learn everything at once.

If you play White and would rather skip this fight, keep in mind that the [Italian Game](artigo:abertura-italiana) only happens when Black answers 1...e5. Against the Sicilian you'll need a plan of your own.

:::pratique Practice the Sicilian
Play Black against the computer and answer 1.e4 with 1...c5. On Hard, you'll feel how much every tempo is worth in these positions.
:::

## Frequently asked questions {#faq "FAQ"}

### Which Sicilian line should I start with?

There's no single answer. The Dragon has plans that are easy to grasp (play down the c-file, bishop on g7). The Najdorf is more flexible but needs more theory.

### Why is the Sicilian considered aggressive?

Because it creates imbalance from the first move. Each side ends up with different trumps, and quick draws are less common than after 1...e5.

### What is the Open Sicilian?

It's when White plays Nf3 and d4 and trades the d-pawn for the c-pawn. Most of the famous lines (Najdorf, Dragon, Sveshnikov) come from it.

### How do I meet the Alapin (2.c3)?

The two most common answers are 2...d5, which trades in the center and brings the queen out early, and 2...Nf6, which attacks e4 and leads to positions reminiscent of the Alekhine Defense.
